# Mode: Contact

For any role 4.0+. Applications get you in the queue; a conversation gets you seen.

## Find two to three people

- **The hiring manager** — usually the VP or Head this role reports to. Often findable from the JD's reporting line plus LinkedIn.
- **A peer** — a product leader already in the relevant org. Best source of what the job actually is.
- **A warm path** — anyone from `07-network.md` who connects you to this company or org. A weak tie beats a cold one every time. Check your prior employers, your vendor contacts, and your educational network.

### Finding the warm path on LinkedIn

- **Search the company's People page, filtered by keyword, and read which 1st-degree connection shows up as the shared mutual.** "Connections of" only works for your own 1st-degree connections, so you can't browse a 2nd-degree contact's network. One company-page search usually names the bridge. *Failure: the plan was to browse a former colleague's contacts; he was 2nd degree, so the list was invisible, while the company-page search showed one 1st-degree connection behind nearly every employee.*
- **Budget people searches; LinkedIn's monthly cap is real.** Roughly 15–20 people-search loads in a day can hit it, after which results blur until the monthly reset. Prefer company-page filters over repeated global searches, and stop at the first "monthly limit" banner. *Failure: the cap hit mid-lookup, and the one search that mattered came back blurred for the rest of the month.*

Record them in the tracker's `hiring_manager`, `recruiter` and `warm_contact` columns, names only. Titles, profile links and mutual connections go in `notes`.

## Draft the notes

One per contact type, each under 90 words:

- **To the hiring manager:** the specific problem you think they're solving and one line of evidence you've solved it. One clear ask — a 15-minute conversation, not "any advice."
- **To the peer:** curiosity, not pitch. What's the stack actually like, what's the hardest part of the role. People answer this.
- **To the warm tie:** the shared context first, the ask second, and make the ask easy to decline.

## Rules

- No flattery. No "I hope this finds you well." No "I came across your profile."
- Specific beats polished. A slightly awkward note about their actual product beats a smooth generic one.
- **Referral before application.** Many ATSs credit whichever source touched the candidate first. Have the referrer submit you through the employer's internal tool first, then apply as "employee referral". *Failure: none yet; caught before an application went in ahead of its referral.*
- **You send them.** Never attempt to send, and never draft anything designed to be sent unreviewed.
- Save to the role's file in `tailored/`.
