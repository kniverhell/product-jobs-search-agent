# Mode: Interview Prep — by round

Trigger: "prep me for this interview", "prep the recruiter screen", `/job-search-agent:interview`.

One question runs the whole prep: **what does this person decide, and what do you need from them?** A recruiter screen
decides whether you are viable on logistics. A hiring manager decides whether you can do the job. Prep for the decision,
not for everything you know.

## 0. Name the round first

Recruiter or coordinator screen · hiring manager · peer or panel · executive or final. If the user hasn't said, ask once.
The round sets the depth:

| Round | What they decide | Prep depth |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter or coordinator screen | Logistics and basic fit (comp, availability, location, level), then pass you on | Role at a glance, a 30-second pitch, a few likely questions, questions that close open items. Little capability selling. |
| Hiring manager | Can you do this job, and will you work well for them | The full prep below: stories, the gaps, a point of view on their problem, a 30–45 day plan. |
| Peer or panel | Craft, judgment, collaboration | Behavioral and product-sense questions mapped to stories; how you work with their function. |
| Executive or final | Scope, strategy, risk | Your view of where the business is heading, the hardest tradeoff you owned, the questions that test the mandate. |

## 1. Source hierarchy

1. **The JD** for the role.
2. **The proof-point ledger** (`06-proof-points.md`) for you, with each item's status.
3. **The resume**, only as checked against the ledger. A resume can be stale or wrong; the ledger wins.
4. **Everything else** (earlier chats, an AI summary, something a recruiter said) is a **claim** until checked against 1–3.

Read also the evaluation report, the deep research if it exists, and `05-story-bank.md`.

## 2. Role at a glance

One screen from the JD alone: title and level, who it reports to, scope, location and arrangement, comp if posted, stated
requirements. So nobody scrolls the posting mid-call.

## 3. Verify before you say it

List every fact you might repeat on the call, each labeled:
- **Confirmed** (name the source: JD, ledger, evaluation);
- **Claim** (name where it came from); do not repeat it as fact;
- **Unknown**, with the question that resolves it.

Unverified is labeled, never dropped and never stated as fact. This covers their facts as well as yours: team size,
budget, reporting line, why the role is open. Same rule as unknowns in scoring: a fork with a resolving question.

## 4. Likely questions and short answers

Map the round's likely questions to existing stories in `05-story-bank.md`; most of the work is retrieval, not invention.
Add the questions your own profile invites (a level mismatch, a short tenure, a gap, a change of domain). For the two or
three where your stories are weak or missing, draft skeletons, ask the user to fill the detail, then commit them to the
bank so the next interview is easier.

## 5. The pitch (or the plan)

- **Screen:** one 30-second answer to "tell me about yourself" or "what would you do here", sized to the audience, built
  from the strongest relevant story and checked against section 3.
- **Hiring manager:** the 30–45 day plan, and a point of view on the company's actual domain problem from the deep
  research.

## 6. Anchors from the ledger

A few proof points, with the exact figure and its status, so numbers are never improvised. Use each by its status:
Solid as stated; Derived only as labeled arithmetic; Soft hedged; Confidential only in its approved directional phrasing;
Designed-only only with its caveat attached; Open and Closed-as-qualitative never as a number. A figure that is on the
resume but not in the ledger is not an anchor: flag it.

## 7. Questions to ask

Five, drawn from the research. **Each one resolves an open item in section 3**, so the call closes the gaps. Not "what's
the culture like."

## 8. JD match

Requirements against ledger evidence, requirement by requirement. Include the exposure list from block B of the
evaluation: every gap will be probed, so have an honest bridge for each, and pick the one gap to lead with.

## Rules

- **Never fill in a blank.** Personal facts the user hasn't given stay blank. The other side's facts stay Unknown.
- **Interviewer research is pasted in by the user.** The agent never opens, searches or reads LinkedIn (see
  `<kit>/modes/contact.md`). If the user wants interviewer background, ask them to paste it. Names and notes about
  interviewers go in the tracker row (`hiring_manager`, `recruiter`, `notes`) or the user's own files, never in the kit.
- **Nothing is sent, booked or submitted for the user.**

## After the interview

Ask what came up that you weren't ready for, and put it in the bank. **The bank is the compounding asset in this whole
system:** the more interviews logged, the harder you become to surprise.
