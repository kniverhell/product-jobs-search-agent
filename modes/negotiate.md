# Mode: Negotiate

Triggered by an offer, a comp question in a screen, or "what should I ask for."

## Before anything else
Read the comp floors in `config.md` and confirm which one applies — they're keyed to days on-site. Assume base salary unless stated otherwise.

**The floor is not the ask.** It's the walk-away. The ask should sit meaningfully above it and be anchored on market data for the level, not on your own minimum. Never let the floor become the anchor in a conversation — the most common way a floor turns into a ceiling is by mentioning it.

**Surface the market comparison when an offer lands.** The negotiate mode should present comp market data for this level, company stage, and geography, so the decision is made against real numbers rather than the floor you set while between roles.

## Research
Level-appropriate comp for this company, stage, and geography. Base, bonus, equity, and what the equity is actually likely worth given stage. Note where the number is a real market read versus a guess, and say which.

## Prep
- **Your number** — the ask, not the floor. Anchored above the target, defensible on market data.
- **The walk-away** — the number below which you say no, decided *before* the conversation, when you're calm.
- **Non-comp levers** — title, scope, direct reports, remote guarantee in writing, start date, review timing, equity refresh. Often more winnable than base, and title/scope compounds into the next role.
- **Their likely pushback** and your response: budget constraints, band limits, geographic discount, internal equity. Have a line for each.

## Rules
- Never give a number first if it can be avoided. If pressed, give a range whose bottom is your target.
- Never negotiate in the moment. "Let me look at the full package and come back to you tomorrow" is always available and always fine.
- This is information and preparation, not advice about what to accept. That decision belongs to the candidate and depends on things not in these files.
